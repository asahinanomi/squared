# squared's heroes

_last updated Oct 26, 2026_

What squared is best at. Come back to this whenever squared grows: every new thing should make a hero stronger, or it doesn't belong.

## The three heroes

1. **The brush: painting your day.** No typing, no mood faces: you paint. Physical, a little slow, honest. The moment people remember.
2. **The zoom out: a life in days.** Two pinches and today is one small square among every day you'll have. The urgency, felt, not told.
3. **The question: "am I spending Today meaningfully?"** The voice of squared. Not how you feel, but what you did with your life today.

**The supporting cast:** moments feed the brush; the paintings reward it.

## How it should feel

Minimal, super pleasant, easy. You feel drawn to it, not pushed. Calm, never techy.

## The test

Before adding anything, ask: which hero does this serve?

- The day points to the brush.
- The zoom points to your life.
- My days points to what you've painted.

If it serves none, it goes (stats, counts, extra rows). If it competes with a hero, it goes.

## Decided so far

- **The paint is gouache, dabbed:** soft round dabs with bristle dots, opaque, matte, settling into one flat coat. Not watercolour (colours would blur into each other). **Never change the brush** without nomi asking.
- **The day's word** ("worth it", "regret", "a blur") signs the lower right corner, like an artist's signature, in a deeper shade of its paint (dark, not white).
- **The zoom out's line is "You are here."** (debated Oct 27, 2026: over "This is today." and "Here, today."). Three words; the picture does the urgency, the words only put you in it. Never longer.
- **Never:** streaks, badges, scores, feeds, dashboards of numbers.

## Built

- **The paint dries** (lab row 6, A3): your textured strokes stay as painted, then soften into one smooth colour as they dry, and the word signs the corner with its sound. No gloss, no darker "wet" step (it read as a blink). The brush itself never changes.
- **A life fills in, every time** (lab row 7): the lived days fill in to today, a wave of grey even with nothing painted, then "You are here." Slower the first time; a touch skips it.
- **No counts in my days.**
