# Beta emails

The emails for the first testers: 10–15 friends nomi trusts. squared is not explained on purpose, to see whether it
makes sense on its own.

**Timing:** the invite now, a tiny nudge around day 4 (so nobody forgets to start), the questions on day 14 (long
enough to see whether it becomes a habit, short enough that they remember their first days).

## 1. The invite (send now)

**Subject:** will you try something I made?

> Hi [name],
>
> I've been making a small app called squared, and I'd love for you to be one of the first to try it.
>
> I won't tell you much about it on purpose. I want to see if it makes sense on its own.
>
> Just open **squared.today** on your phone, sign in with Google, and use it however feels natural, ideally once a day,
> for two weeks. (Tip: add it to your home screen so it feels like an app.)
>
> In two weeks I'll send you five quick questions. Your honest answers will help me more than anything.
>
> Thank you, truly.
>
> — nomi

## 2. The nudge (day 4, optional)

> Hi [name], a quick hello from squared. Have you had a chance to open it yet? No pressure, just a nudge. 🙂 — nomi

## 3. The questions (day 14)

**Subject:** five quick questions about squared

> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for living with squared for two weeks. Five quick questions, a sentence or two each is perfect:
>
> 1. In your own words, what do you think squared is for?
> 2. What 3 things did you like or use the most?
> 3. What 3 things confused you, annoyed you, or felt missing?
> 4. Would you keep using it? Why, or why not?
> 5. How did it feel to use it: happy, calm, sad, guilty, something else?
>
> And if there was a moment you got stuck, tell me where. That's gold.
>
> — nomi

## Why these questions

- **1** is the intuitiveness test: compare their answer with what squared really does.
- **3** says "confused" as well as "annoyed": that's where intuitiveness problems show.
- **5** asks how it felt, not just happy or sad: "guilty" (a red day) is the feeling to catch early.
- **Where you got stuck** is often the most useful line anyone sends.

## Answers

Keep what comes back here (or a link to it), one line per person: their answer to question 1, and anything that
repeats across people.
