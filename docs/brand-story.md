## 1. One line

squared is a daily conscience tracker. Each day you paint one square, one honest answer to **"did I spend Today meaningfully?"** Zoom out, and the squares show the shape of your life.

## 2. The idea (the why)

- The **practice** is awareness: noticing your days *as you live them*.
- The **goal** is a meaningful day: spent on what matters to you.
- The **enemy** is the day that slips past unnoticed. (The one that made this app exist.)

**Meaningful, not good.** Life is meaningful in some very bad days, and it's not just about having good days. (nomi, Oct 23, 2026: the day her dad died, she was there and said she forgave him. Green.) That's part of why squared exists, and why it never becomes a good-day / bad-day mood tracker.

**It makes you think deeply.** Painting one square asks for a moment of honest reflection: what did this day hold, and was I there for it? That pause, thinking deeply about your own days, is itself meaningful. It's what squared does for people. (nomi, Oct 23, 2026, painting her own past days: "this practice makes me think deeply, and that is meaningful.")

**Less regret.** I want to live meaningfully because I can't bear the thought of the regret days. On my death bed, I want to say I have no regrets, or fewer. squared helps me see them while there's still time to choose differently. (nomi, Oct 23, 2026)

Rooted in Covey's urgent-vs-important: a day can be busy and still not matter. squared asks you to notice the difference, one day at a time.

**The metaphor: black and white, then colour** (the Wizard of Oz, when Dorothy steps out into colour). squared is ink on paper; the only colour anywhere is what you paint. Days stay paper until you paint them, so your life literally turns colourful as you go. Celebrate days "in colour", never days "missed".

## 3. The model (why there are exactly three colours)

Every day answers two hidden questions:
1. **Were you awake to it?** Present, or on autopilot.
2. **Did it go to what matters?** Meaningful, or busywork.

| | went to what matters | didn't |
|---|---|---|
| **you were there** | 🟩 worth it | 🟥 regret |
| **on autopilot** | — | ⬜ a blur |

🟩 and 🟥 are siblings: both days you *lived*. ⬜ is the outlier: the day you weren't there for. Grey still counts as "in colour": a blurry day was still noticed, afterwards.

## 4. The three colours — LOCKED

Use the **one-liner** wherever there's room. Use the **short label** in tight UI. Never invent new phrasings.

| colour | short label | one-liner (palette, the meaningful sheet) |
|---|---|---|
| 🟩 green | **worth it** | yes, I spent it on what matters. |
| 🟥 red | **regret** | no, I wish I'd spent it differently. |
| ⬜ grey | **a blur** | I don't know… I wasn't paying attention. |

The same three lines everywhere: the palette, the meaningful sheet and the letter (why squared).

**Why these words (Oct 23, 2026).** Ask a friend "how was your day?" and they'd answer "worth it", "a regret" or "a blur": the labels sound like that honest answer. Red is the day you were there for and wish you'd chosen differently; grey is the day you weren't there for. Psychology behind it: regret is a useful feeling when it points at the act ("I wish I'd…"), never at the person, and paired with kindness it moves people to choose better. In counts: "3 worth it", "2 regrets", "1 blur"; just painted: "your 3rd day worth it this month", "your 2nd regret", "your 1st blur".

Voice rule: all three one-liners are **first person** (it's your own conscience, not the app grading you) and follow the skeleton **[yes, / no, / I don't know…] [honest clause].**

## 5. The daily question — LOCKED

> **did I spend Today meaningfully?**

- **Its tense follows the day** (Oct 24, 2026). Today, until it's painted: *am I spending Today meaningfully?*, a nudge to be present while it's lived. Once today is painted: *did I spend Today meaningfully?*, the honest look back. Any other day: *did I spend this day meaningfully?* Sign-in, onboarding and the letter keep the classic *did I spend Today meaningfully?*
- First person. Always.
- **Today** is capitalised, with a dotted underline: it's the day itself, any day you're looking at.
- Set in the reflective voice (Fraunces). It's the hero of the day screen, sign-in and onboarding.
- The three colours are its three honest answers.
- "meaningful" is squared's north-star word: it lives in the question and the meaningful sheet, never as a colour label.

## 6. "Meaningful" — the definition (the meaningful sheet)

> **meaningful**
> Not a good day or a bad one. Not what you got done. A day is meaningful when you spent it on what matters to you.

Then the three one-liners (section 4).

## 8. Voice principles

- **Squares and days.** A **square** is the thing you paint: "paint its square", "start over under the square", "how many squares do you have?", "your squares and notes". A **day** is time you live: "days in colour", "10,961 days ahead", "my days", "framed days". A place named for looking back says days; a thing you touch with the brush says square.

- First person, plain-spoken. Honest, never preachy.
- No streaks. No scores. No judgment from the app, only your own. Counts are days in colour, never days missed.
- Warm, quiet, a little tender. It's a conscience, not a coach.
- Say the honest thing simply ("I wasn't paying attention") rather than dressing it up.
- **No long dashes (—) in copy**: they read as written by AI. Use a full stop, a comma, a colon or "…". The one exception is the signature, "— nomi".
- Copy rules: statements in sentence case; buttons, controls and helper text lowercase; "I" always capitalised; "squared" always lowercase.
- Signed **— nomi** where a human voice appears (the idea page, thanks for feedback).
- Never techy: no dashboards of numbers, legends or stats for their own sake.
